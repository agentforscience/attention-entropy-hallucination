\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\para{The direction of the effect.}
In the upper layers, attention is slightly \emph{more} concentrated when the answer is wrong, the opposite of the ``looking around'' intuition.
One possible reading is that when the model lacks the fact, upper-layer heads have less relevant material to integrate and fall back on a few fixed positions, whereas a retrieved fact spreads attention over the subject and relation tokens.
We did not test this mechanism.
It is a hypothesis for follow-up work, not a finding.

\para{Why pooled numbers mislead.}
Domains differ at once in prompt style, in attention entropy, and in the base rate of error.
Any feature that separates domains will therefore look like a hallucination detector on pooled data.
In our data a five-way domain indicator reaches 0.72 to 0.79 AUROC with no information about the answer at all.
We recommend that studies of this kind report a domain-only baseline and a within-domain breakdown by default.

\para{Relation to prior work.}
Our results agree with the general picture that single-pass internal signals carry correctness information, but that output probabilities are a strong baseline for short-answer QA.
That per-head features do much better than per-layer averages fits the head-level approach of Lookback Lens \citep{chuang2024lookback}.

\para{Practical implication.}
Layer-wise attention entropy is not a good stand-alone hallucination detector for short factual answers.
Token likelihood from the same forward pass is stronger, and entropy adds about 0.01 AUROC on top of it.

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\para{Domain is confounded with dataset.}
Science and trivia come from different sources than the three \popqa domains, with different question styles and label quality.
\Hthree comparisons that involve science may reflect \sciq rather than science as a subject.

\para{Label noise.}
By our 60-example audit, roughly 3--8\% of labels are wrong or debatable, and judge and string match disagree most in science.
Noise lowers every AUROC and could contribute to the science dip.
Results agree across all three labelings.

\para{Deviations from the plan.}
We made several changes after the plan was written, listed in full in \secref{app:deviations}.
Three matter for interpretation.
First, the judge is an 8B model rather than the planned 14B model.
Second, we redefined the primary label as a hybrid of judge and string match after auditing judge errors; we made this choice after seeing provisional string-match-only results but before any judge-based result.
Third, we added the domain-only control and the within-domain reading of \Htwo after a provisional run exposed the confound, so that part of the analysis is not pre-specified.

\para{Task scope.}
We study short-answer closed-book QA with answers of a few tokens.
We did not test long-form generation, summarization, or retrieval-augmented settings, and entropy during generation may behave differently there.

\para{Models.}
We test two instruction-tuned models of 7--8B parameters and no larger models.

\para{Small factual class.}
\qwen answers only 88 creative-works questions correctly, so estimates for that domain are wide.

\para{Feature definition.}
We take entropy over all attended positions, including chat-template and system-prompt tokens, and did not examine entropy restricted to question tokens.
We fixed classifier regularization at $C=1$ and did not tune it.
